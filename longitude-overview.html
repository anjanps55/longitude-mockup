Page 01 — The Problem

Modern medicine is extraordinary. Access to understanding it is not.

The gap between what doctors know and what patients can act on is where outcomes are lost. Longitude exists to close that gap.

The literacy gap

Most people cannot fully advocate for themselves in a clinical setting

Health information is asymmetric by default — the patient has the most at stake and the least context. This is not an American problem, an Indian problem, or a problem of any one health system. It is the mainstream experience of being a patient almost everywhere.

Why this matters

Advocacy works — but today it is a privilege

Patients who self-advocate get measurably better outcomes: fewer misdiagnoses, earlier interventions, treatments better matched to their biology. But advocacy requires three things most people do not have:

  • Knowledge — knowing which questions and tests actually matter for your situation.
  • Confidence — the standing to ask them, and to push back when an answer feels thin.
  • Access — the information that makes a question worth asking in the first place.

Today that combination correlates with income, education, and personal connections. Longitude's purpose is to decouple advocacy from privilege — to give every patient the preparation that today only the most resourced ones have.

Why this is personal

Founder Story

For Anjan, this began at home. His mother fought a lifelong battle with kidney disease — and never came to know what caused her kidneys to fail. She went on to live 19 years with a transplanted kidney, holding that ground year after year by advocating relentlessly for herself. She showed Anjan, long before he had words for it, what informed self-advocacy is worth to a patient: time.

Anjan was later diagnosed with an extremely rare kidney disease of his own. His nephrologist recommended steroid therapy. Through self-directed research, Anjan found a genetic panel that revealed he was genetically resistant to steroids — the recommended treatment would have been life-compromising.

His uncle, diagnosed with colorectal cancer, similarly had to self-advocate for molecular testing and clinical-trial access that changed his treatment path. Most patients would never know to ask.

It did not stop with family. In the years since, Anjan has walked many friends through the same kind of moment — personally helping each of them build the advocacy pathway that brought them through. Longitude turns that repeated, hard-won work into a product, so finding the right path no longer depends on knowing the right person.

Why now

The pieces needed to solve this only recently fell into place

Patient advocacy has always mattered. What is new is that it is finally buildable at scale — four shifts have converged in the last few years:

  • Health data is becoming portable. Interoperability standards and national data-exchange networks mean a patient's records can finally be assembled in one place.
  • AI can now do the synthesis. Reasoning across labs, genetics, guidelines, and trials in plain language was not possible at consumer quality until recently.
  • Patients expect digital health. Post-pandemic, managing care through an app is normal, not novel.
  • The competitive gap is open. No platform yet combines longitudinal multi-modal data with AI advocacy — and the nearest competitor is scoped to rare disease only. The window is real but finite.
Page 02 — The Solution

A longitudinal health record that works for the patient.

Longitude is an AI-powered patient health advocacy platform. It does not replace the doctor — it makes the patient ready for the doctor.

In one sentence

Product Definition

Longitude aggregates a person's biometrics, labs, medications, genetics, wellness signals, and clinical records into one longitudinal profile — then uses AI to generate personalised advocacy intelligence: what to ask doctors, what tests to request, what trials are relevant, and what genetic insights could change a treatment decision.

The model

Three pillars: Aggregate, Analyze, Advocate

Every Longitude feature sits on one of three steps. The first two are increasingly commoditised. The third is the product.

PILLAR 01

Aggregate

Labs, medications, genetics, biometrics, wellness data, and clinical records — pulled into one longitudinal view that follows the patient over years, not visits.

PILLAR 02

Analyze

AI cross-references the patient's full picture against clinical literature, treatment guidelines, genetic databases, and trial registries — and detects what's trending, missing, or worth a second look.

PILLAR 03

Advocate

The output the patient actually uses: questions to ask, tests to request, trials to explore, and insights a rushed appointment may have missed — every one cited and explainable.

The wedge

What makes Longitude defensible

The advocacy layer

Data aggregation, consumer genomics, and trial matching are all commoditising. The intelligence layer that synthesises them into "based on everything about you, here's what to do" is not.

Longitudinal multi-modal convergence

No platform unifies clinical records + labs + biometrics + genomics + nutrition + medications into one timeline and generates advocacy from it. Longitude integrates all six.

The medication–food–genetics nexus

A triple convergence no competitor offers: how a patient's genetics, their current drugs, and their diet interact — surfaced as one safety and optimisation layer.

Patient-owned, enterprise-funded

The patient controls their data — avoiding the employer-owned-data trap. Revenue comes from B2B2C channels, avoiding the B2C-only model that has sunk consumer-health companies.

Boundaries

What Longitude is not — out of scope for v1

Drawing the line clearly is itself a safety decision. Longitude is an education and advocacy tool, not a diagnostic device or care provider.

Not diagnosis or treatment. Every recommendation routes the patient back to their provider.

Not telemedicine. No virtual consultations — we prepare patients for their own visits.

Not a pharmacy. No prescription ordering or fulfilment.

Not a claims processor. Claims data is shown for context only.

Not imaging analysis. Radiology reports are read as text; raw scans are not.

Not a social network. Community insight is anonymised and aggregated — no messaging or forums.

What Longitude Lite is

Zero setup. One question. One honest answer.

Lite is an instant way to ask a single health question and get a calibrated reply — is this fine, should I see someone, or do I need help now? No account, no data to connect, no profile to build. It is the acute-triage instinct of the platform, exposed directly.

What Lite is

A fast, friendly answer to one everyday worry — built around triage and escalation, written in plain language, honest about uncertainty.

What Lite is not

Not a profile, not a data platform, not a symptom-list generator. It never tells a user what they "have" — it tells them what to do next.

A dull ache near the belly button a day after a new food is most often simple indigestion or a mild stomach bug. Here is exactly what would change that assessment:

  • Pain moves to the lower-right and sharpens — could be appendicitis; get seen today.
  • Fever, persistent vomiting, or any blood — get seen now.

If you do see someone, two questions worth asking: "Could this be more than indigestion?" and "What should bring me back sooner?"

Why Lite belongs in Longitude

Lite acquires. The platform retains.

The everyday-question user is the mass market — easy to reach, costs nothing emotionally to try. The chronic-condition user is rare, hard to reach cold, and the one who monetises. Lite is the cheap, viral top-of-funnel; Longitude is the deep, paid retention product. The upgrade is honest, never forced:

  1. A user asks Lite an everyday question — no account, no setup.
  2. Lite answers, calibrated, with escalation built in. They leave satisfied.
  3. Over time, Lite notices a pattern — the same symptom returning, a new diagnosis, a medication to manage.
  4. Lite invites them up: "Want Longitude to remember this, so the next answer already knows your history?" — the step into the full profile.
Recommendation

Build Lite as a mode within Longitude, not a separate company. A standalone symptom-answer app is the most commoditised space in digital health and has no moat of its own — and a pre-seed team cannot afford two brands, two go-to-markets, and a split focus. One brand, one engine, two front doors: Lite is how people meet Longitude; the longitudinal platform is how they stay.

Safety

The highest-stakes surface gets the most caution

Acute, one-off symptom triage is the highest-liability thing Longitude does — a stomach ache is occasionally appendicitis; "is this just indigestion" is occasionally something serious. Lite leans entirely on triage and escalation, never diagnosis or default reassurance. Because Lite has no longitudinal history to reason against, it is deliberately tuned to be more conservative than the full platform: when genuinely uncertain, it routes the user toward care. The escalation framework that governs this is detailed on Page 08.

Page 04 — The Intelligence Model

How Longitude turns data into advocacy.

Aggregating health data is the easy part. The hard part — and the product — is the model that reads it and reasons about it. Nine principles define how Longitude thinks.

The premise

A health platform can store a patient's data and show it back to them. That is a filing cabinet. Longitude is built to do something harder: reason about the data the way a brilliant, well-prepared doctor would — one who knows the patient's whole history, has read the current literature, and is firmly on the patient's side.

That behaviour is not an accident of good prompts. It is a deliberate design model — nine principles, each shaping how an insight is generated, checked, and delivered. Every module in this document is an application of the principles below.

The nine principles

01

The Specialist Model

The principle

Health advocacy is not one job. Preparing for a visit, interpreting a result, scouting research, and triaging a worry each demand a different kind of expert — so the engine runs as a team of focused specialists, not one generalist.

In the product

The AI Advocacy Engine is that team behind one interface. Visit Briefing, "What This Means", trial matching, and Ask Longitude are each driven by a specialist tuned to its single job.

02

The Calibrated Voice

The principle

The right tone is a battle-hardened ER doctor's — calibrated, not cautious; warm, not clinical; honest about uncertainty. It gives real assessments instead of hiding behind "consult your doctor".

In the product

One communication standard across every screen, alert, and message. Real conclusions delivered with warmth — never hedged into uselessness, never coldly clinical.

03

Red / Yellow / Green Escalation

The principle

Every assessment is triaged into three calibrated levels — act now, talk to your doctor soon, or you're okay. De-escalation is taken as seriously as escalation: a tool that warns about everything is trusted for nothing.

In the product

The logic behind every alert and lab flag — and the spine of Longitude Lite. Severity is never decorative; it tells the patient whether to act, ask, or relax. Detailed on Page 08.

04

Separating the Read from the Delivery

The principle

A clinical assessment should be produced dispassionately and accurately first; the empathetic, plain-language delivery is a second, separate step. Neither is allowed to compromise the other.

In the product

"The read" and "the delivery" are generated separately, so the patient gets accuracy that isn't softened away and warmth that isn't an afterthought.

05

Source-First Trust

The principle

Nothing is asserted from thin air. Every claim traces to a real document — a lab value, a genetic variant, a guideline, a study — and the patient's own source records are never altered.

In the product

A "show the source" affordance on every insight. Each recommendation cites exactly what it is based on. No black-box outputs.

06

The Living Knowledge Base

The principle

A health record should be a living, interlinked knowledge base — collected, organised, and cross-referenced over time — not a pile of disconnected documents the patient has to assemble themselves.

In the product

This is the Longitudinal Health Profile. Longitude continuously gathers, structures, and interlinks the patient's health story so the whole picture is always one step away.

07

Evidence Tiers

The principle

Not all evidence is equal, and patients deserve to know which is which. Every claim is labelled by quality — from clinically validated down to community-reported — and weaker evidence is labelled, never hidden.

In the product

Every recommendation carries a visible evidence tier, so a patient can tell "proven" from "promising" without a medical degree. The full tier system is on Page 08.

08

The Advocacy Plan

The principle

Advocacy works best as a single, ruthlessly prioritised, dated plan — tiered from "your specialist would prescribe this" down to "worth watching" — that always shows what changed since the last version.

In the product

A proactive Advocacy Plan: a living, prioritised set of next steps that updates as the profile changes and highlights what's new every time.

09

Compounding Intelligence

The principle

A patient's questions and discoveries should make the system permanently smarter. Insight from one conversation should not vanish into a chat log — it should fold back into the record.

In the product

Every meaningful interaction can be saved into the profile. The more a patient uses Longitude, the more personal and precise it becomes.

Why the model is the moat

Strategic note

Data aggregation, consumer genomics, and trial-matching are all becoming commodities — any well-funded team can buy or build them. The nine principles above are not a feature anyone can copy from a screenshot; they are how the product reasons and earns trust. That is where Longitude's defensibility compounds over time — and why the intelligence model, not the data plumbing, is the centre of this document.

Page 07 — Flows

Four journeys where Longitude changes the conversation.

Each journey has a trigger, a flow, and a success state — the moment the patient walks into an appointment more prepared than they have ever been.

1

New Diagnosis Journey

Trigger: the patient receives a new diagnosis — entered manually or detected from clinical records.
  1. The diagnosis is entered into Longitude.
  2. A Condition Briefing surfaces immediately: key facts, genetic components, standard-of-care tests, treatment landscape, relevant trials.
  3. Longitude generates "First Questions" — specific questions for the diagnosing physician.
  4. If genetic testing is relevant, it flags: "consider asking about this before starting treatment."
  5. Continuous clinical-trial scanning begins for the condition.
  6. The patient reviews, saves questions, and optionally shares with a caregiver.
Success state: the patient arrives at the next appointment with evidence-based questions that change the treatment conversation — exactly the conversation Anjan had to fight to have.
Engine Condition Intelligence, generated by the Patient Advocate specialist.
2

Lab Results Review Journey

Trigger: new lab results arrive — via a connected source or a manual photo/PDF upload.
  1. Longitude parses and normalises the lab values.
  2. Each value is shown with a plain-language interpretation.
  3. The result is placed in longitudinal context — the current value against every prior result.
  4. Concerning values or trends are flagged with calibrated severity ("your eGFR has declined from 72 to 58 over 18 months").
  5. If warranted, advocacy recommendations are generated for the next visit.
  6. Results are cross-referenced with medications and genetics for interaction insights.
Success state: the patient understands their results in context — and knows whether, and what, to discuss with their doctor.
Engine The Results Interpreter specialist — signal separated from noise.
3

Doctor Visit Preparation Journey

Trigger: the patient has an upcoming appointment — entered manually or synced from a calendar.
  1. Longitude analyses the full profile: recent labs, medication changes, symptom trends, pending results.
  2. It generates a prioritised Visit Briefing — 3–5 key discussion points.
  3. Each point comes with specific questions and evidence citations.
  4. A one-page patient summary can be exported to share with the provider.
  5. After the visit, the patient logs outcomes, new orders, and referrals.
  6. The profile updates and future recommendations adjust accordingly.
Success state: a 3–5 minute review means the patient addresses the issues that a rushed appointment would otherwise have missed.
Engine The Patient Advocate specialist builds the appointment agenda.
4

Clinical Trial Discovery Journey

Trigger: a background scan detects a new matching trial — or the patient actively searches.
  1. Longitude identifies a trial matching the patient's condition, profile, location, and eligibility.
  2. It presents a summary: what the trial tests, why the patient may qualify, where it is, what phase.
  3. Specific questions for the patient's specialist are generated.
  4. If the patient is interested, contact and enrolment guidance is provided.
  5. Trial status is tracked, with alerts if enrolment is closing.
Success state: a patient discovers a relevant trial their community physician never mentioned — exactly the gap Anjan's uncle had to bridge alone.
Engine The R&D Scout specialist — research made personal.
Page 08 — The Trust Layer

The safety framework is not a feature. It's the foundation.

A platform that hands patients medical recommendations must earn trust deliberately. Longitude's safety model is built in from the first screen — it is not a setting, and it is not optional.

Escalation framework

Every assessment is triaged — calibrated, never alarmist

Severity always tells the patient one of three things. De-escalation is treated as seriously as escalation: a tool that says "call your doctor" for everything stops being trusted.

Red — Act now

Clear, urgent, unsoftened. "Stop reading this and get emergency care. Here's exactly what to tell the triage nurse."

Yellow — Soon

Calm but clear. "This doesn't need the ER, but talk to your doctor in the next day or two. Here's why, and what to tell them."

Green — You're okay

Warm and specific. "Here's why this is normal — and here's exactly what would change my advice."

Mental-health crisis protocol

If distress surfaces, support comes first

If a user expresses suicidal ideation, self-harm, or severe psychological distress, Longitude acknowledges it warmly and immediately, surfaces crisis resources — 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741), 911 — and then still engages with their health question. Both things are true at once: the person needs support and they have a legitimate medical concern. The product never refuses to help.

How disclaimers work

Honest about limits — without hiding behind them

What Longitude does

Weaves its limits into the conversation naturally — "I can help you prepare and understand, but I'm not your doctor and I can't examine you." Honest, early, human.

What Longitude refuses to do

Stamp a legal banner on every screen, append "consult your doctor" to every paragraph, or use disclaimers as an excuse to give vague, useless answers.

Source-first & evidence tiers

Every claim shows its work

No black-box outputs. Each recommendation cites the lab value, genetic variant, guideline, or study behind it — and carries a visible evidence tier so the patient can weigh it correctly.

TIER 1 Clinically Validated

Strong evidence from randomised trials, meta-analyses, or firm clinical consensus.

TIER 2 Active Clinical Trials

Currently in human trials — phase and recruitment status shown.

TIER 3 Early Research

Published but not yet in human trials, or very early human data.

TIER 4 Theoretical / Horizon

A plausible mechanism, but no direct evidence yet — worth watching, not acting on.

TIER 5 Community / Anecdotal

Patient-reported, source always linked. Valuable signal — clearly labelled, never proof.

Regulatory posture

Position

Longitude is deliberately scoped as an education and advocacy tool, not a diagnostic or decision-support device. It never diagnoses, never prescribes, and always routes the patient back to a licensed provider. This boundary — set on Page 02 — is what keeps the product on the right side of the line while still being genuinely useful.

Page 09 — Experience

Health is complex. The experience should feel calm.

Longitude's design exists to reduce anxiety, not amplify it — warm and editorial where most health products are cold and clinical.

Design principles

🎯

Clarity over density

Health data is inherently complex. Every screen answers one primary question.

🔍

Progressive disclosure

Start simple — "your kidney function is declining" — then let the patient drill into the trend, the guidelines, and the questions.

🔎

Trust through transparency

Show the source for every recommendation. Never hide the reasoning behind a confident-looking answer.

🕊️

Calm urgency

Urgent flags are clear but never panic-inducing. Communicate with colour and hierarchy, not exclamation marks.

♿

Accessible by default

Plain language at a sixth-grade reading level, high contrast, scalable text, screen-reader support. Clinical detail is always available on demand — never forced.
